This interactive multiplot compares greenhouse gas emissions per person and population in Brazil, China and India from 2000 to 2023.

Interactive visualisation

Figure 1. Emissions trends and population–emissions comparison. The selected scatter plot year is 2023. Data from Jones et al. (2025), Our World in Data (2024, n.d.) and United Nations (2024).

Exercise summary

I reused the Module 7 dataset for Brazil, China and India from 2000 to 2023, checked for missing values and duplicate records, and converted the population data into millions. Using R and Plotly, I combined the emissions trend chart with the new population–emissions scatter plot. Both panels use consistent country colours and emissions scales. The hover label shows the values, the legend controls the visibility of the countries in both panels, and the year slider updates the scatter plot. In 2023, India had the largest population and the lowest per capita emissions among the three countries, while Brazil showed the opposite pattern.

Population and greenhouse gas emissions in Brazil, China and India, 2000–2023
Summary

The interactive multiplot combines the emissions trend chart and the new population–emissions scatter plot. The line chart shows the full time period, and the year slider is used to select the year displayed in the scatter plot. The shared legend controls the visibility of the countries in the two panels.

Mapping

Panel A: The horizontal axis shows the year (2000–2023), and the vertical axis shows the greenhouse gas emissions in carbon dioxide equivalents per person calculated according to the 100-year warming timescale (unit: tonnes). The emissions include land use emissions. The data comes from Jones et al. (2025) and is processed by Our World in Data.

Panel B: The horizontal axis represents the population (unit: millions), and the vertical axis represents the same emissions measure. The population data comes from the saved Module 7 dataset, which is sourced from Our World in Data (2024) and the United Nations (2024). Each point represents a country in the selected year.

Brazil, China and India are shown in purple, blue and brown in both panels. The trend lines are solid, dashed and dotted respectively. The scatter markers are circles, squares and diamonds respectively. Text labels are used to identify countries in the scatter plot.

The slider can select one of the 24 years, only change the scatter plot, and the complete trend chart remains visible. Click on the country in the legend to hide or show the country in the two panels. The hover label shows the year, emissions (rounded to one decimal place), and the population in Panel B (rounded to one decimal place, in millions).
Important Notes

The saved Module 7 dataset contains 72 observations from three countries from 2000 to 2023. No missing values or duplicate country-year records were found. The population has been converted into millions. The two emissions axes are still fixed between 0 and 25 tonnes per person, and the population axis is fixed between 0 and 1,600 million. Emissions include land use emissions, which are estimates and may need to be revised. National averages conceal differences within countries, and the three-country comparison cannot establish a causal relationship between population and emissions.
